# Hooks

The hook is the first 1–3 s: one spoken line, one frame-0 picture, and a promise the film keeps. Frame 0 is also the thumbnail and, on vertical feeds, what the loop returns to. It decides whether anyone sees the rest.

## The rules

- **Frame 0 is never empty.** The real app is visible, with a headline of 3–6 words readable in the first frame. No black frame, no lone logo, no blank canvas that fades in. Motion starts on frame 0.
- **The voice starts at once.** The first word comes 0.2–0.4 s in (the timeline `lead`), and the hook line is finished by 3 s. That is 10 words at most.
- **It opens a loop.** It raises a question, a gap or a promise that only the payoff (60–80% in) closes. The payoff must actually deliver it; an unkept hook loses the viewer and their trust.
- **It's specific.** Use a real noun from the app (the exam's name, the score screen, the actual button), a real number from the source, or the viewer's real situation. Generic hooks ("Want to be more productive?") get scrolled past.
- **Never open with:** "Introducing", "Meet", "Hey guys", "In this video", the logo, a greeting, a loading screen, a login screen, or typing into an empty prompt box with nothing else on screen.
- **Name the audience** when the app is for a specific person ("Taking the AWS Solutions Architect exam this month?"). It makes the right viewer stay and is fine to lose the rest.

## Patterns

Write five hooks from five different patterns, then score them. The examples are for a practice-exam app; replace every noun with your app's real ones.

| Pattern | Spoken line | Frame 0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Result first** | "Eighty-nine percent. Three weeks ago this was forty-one." | The real score screen at 1.8×, the number filling a third of the frame |
| **The pain moment** | "Exam's in six hours. You still don't know VPC peering." | A phone at night: the time and the topic list, one item red |
| **Question with the input** | "What if every question you missed came back until you got it?" | A missed question in the real UI, already sliding back into the queue |
| **The number lands** | "Two thousand practice questions. One of them is your weak spot." | The question count from the app, rolling, then one card pulled out |
| **The feature as the hook** | "Watch it find your weakest topic." | The app mid-action: the topic chart re-sorting itself |
| **Audience callout** | "Studying for SAA-C03? Stop re-reading the docs." | The exam badge and a stack of docs being swept aside by the app |
| **Before and after** | "This is how you studied. This is how you'll study." | Split frame: a wall of PDF tabs, and the app's review screen; one side wins |
| **Contrarian** | "Don't take another full practice exam." | A full 65-question exam crossed out; the app's 10-question drill beside it |

## Scoring

Score each hook from 0 to 2 on each line, and keep the two highest scores. On a tie, pick the one with the stronger frame 0.

| Test | 2 points when |
| --- | --- |
| Specific | It names a real thing from the app or a real number from the source |
| Visual | Frame 0 shows the product doing the thing, not a title over a background |
| Gap | A stranger wants to know what happens next |
| Kept | The payoff beat answers it on screen |
| True | Every word traces to the app, its site or the person |
| Fast | It's spoken in under 3 s and its headline is 6 words or fewer |

## Keeping them past the hook

- **A re-hook every 8–12 s.** It's one line that turns toward what comes next: "But here's the part that matters." "Now watch what happens when you miss one." "That took four seconds." It costs one sentence and resets attention before the drop-off points.
- **The spine data travels.** The same real question, topic or file from the hook comes back in every beat until the payoff resolves it. The viewer follows an object, not a feature list.
- **Something new every 2–3 s**, and no foreground holds still for more than 4 s, except the end card (see the retention checklist).
- **The payoff is the biggest moment**, played at normal speed, often silent under a music lift (`# silent:` in its scene file).
- **On vertical, the end loops.** The last frame is thumbnail-grade (topic line, the app visible, the CTA below it) and leads back into frame 0.
